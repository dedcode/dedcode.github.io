How the prediction is made

1. Measure distance. L2 is straight-line distance: √(Δx₁² + Δx₂²). L1 is the sum of coordinate differences: |Δx₁| + |Δx₂|. Both axes use the same units and scale.

2. Find K neighbors. The dashed circle (L2) or diamond (L1) reaches the Kth neighbor. Exactly K examples vote, with equal weight.

3. Count the votes. The class with the most votes wins. Equal largest vote counts are shown as a neutral tie. Equal distances are ordered by point ID, so the choice at the Kth position is reproducible.

Background colors are a sampled view of predictions across the plane; the query and neighbor distances are calculated exactly. A vote fraction is not a calibrated probability. Small K can follow individual examples closely; larger K can smooth away local structure. Changes are kept until you reload. Changing class count, point count, or New samples generates a new dataset.
