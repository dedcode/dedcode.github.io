What is K-means doing?

1. Assign points. Give each point the cluster of its closest centroid using Euclidean distance. The pale regions show which centroid is nearest at each location.

2. Move centroids. Replace each centroid with the mean x₁ and mean x₂ of its assigned points. The point colors stay fixed during this step, until the next assignment.

3. Repeat. Stop when an assignment step changes no labels. Each nonempty cluster has its centroid at the mean. Different starting positions can give different results.

The objective: SSE = Σᵢ ‖xᵢ − μcᵢ‖², the sum of squared distances from each point to its assigned centroid. Each assignment or mean update can only decrease or preserve this value. It can settle at a local optimum.

KNN versus K-means: KNN uses labeled neighbors to predict a label. K-means groups unlabeled data. Here, K means the number of clusters.

K-means++ spreads starting centers by sampling in proportion to squared distance from the closest selected center; it does not guarantee the best result. Distance ties go to the lower-numbered centroid. Empty clusters keep their previous centroid. Both axes have equal scale. Changing data, K, initialization, or coordinates starts a new run; Reset run replays the same starting centers. State lasts until you reload.
